    What this is, and when you would reach for it

    What it is

    A builder for the vocabulary your agent draws with — and for the renderers behind it. Pick the components you allow, point a library at them, and copy out three things you then own: the catalog, the renderer for your stack, and the catalog as types.

    Not a component library with a chat box. No model runs in the render path.

    Who it is for

    Developers whose product renders UI an agent produced, and who do not want it inventing things — including the ones who have to prove that in writing.

    Public services under the European Accessibility Act, enforceable since 28 June 2025, and anyone else who would rather find this before an audit does.

    When you use it

    • Writing the vocabulary — before an agent ever sees it.
    • Adding a stack — one table, not a second renderer.
    • Every answer — in your app, or in CI, on the tree.

    In the browser, in a terminal, or as a check in your pipeline. Nothing to install to try it.

    It looks fine. That is the problem.

    The same answer, twice: what a reader sees, and what the machine is told. A schema checks the first column. Nobody was checking the second.

    You cannot review a screen that is different every time

    So the wrong answer has to be unsayable. Three layers — and only the last one runs per answer.

    1. 1

      The catalog

      What the agent may ask for. Reviewed once, like an API. If it cannot say “heading”, no screen it ever draws has one.

    2. 2

      The binding

      How each component becomes markup in your stack. Certified once, in CI — contrast, target size, focus. Properties of the implementation, not of the request.

    3. 3

      The answer

      What the agent actually sent. Checked every time, on the component tree rather than the markup — so the same rules hold for React, Flutter or SwiftUI.
